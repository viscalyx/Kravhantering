# Actions and choices

Apply these checks only to passages that contain the relevant information.

- Separate action now, background, and optional choices through wording or
  placement. Add labels only when they help the reader distinguish them.
- Make an instruction concrete: who acts, what to do, where and when to do it,
  why it matters, and what shows completion. Supply what the reader needs;
  avoid repeating information already clear from the surrounding text.
- Base requirements on a real prerequisite or constraint. State the condition
  for a conditional step and the benefit of an optional step. Mark noncritical
  checks optional. For a required checklist item, give the verification steps,
  passing result, and factual consequence of skipping it. Describe risks with
  the certainty the evidence supports.
- Follow each applicable path from preparation to outcome. When a step becomes
  optional or changes, repair later instructions that depend on it, including
  affected references within the requested scope. Report dependencies outside
  that scope when they would leave the instructions inconsistent.
- Trace copied values from source to destination. Distinguish the field name,
  displayed label, actual value, and place to paste it. Use synthetic examples
  where useful; keep private values out of examples.
- Match interface instructions to evidence for the reader's version and
  context. Preserve visible labels even when they use another language.
  Investigate conflicts between documentation and observed screens; avoid
  guessing a new navigation path. If evidence remains insufficient, identify
  the missing fact and ask for it before asserting the path.
- Explain unfamiliar behavior in terms of what actually happens: who or what
  starts it, when it happens, and what the reader will notice. Distinguish
  automatic behavior from actions the reader must perform.
- When readers must communicate something, provide usable wording and explain
  why recipients need it. Keep genuine human responsibilities and constraints;
  include agent permissions or implementation details only if they affect the
  reader's task or decision.
